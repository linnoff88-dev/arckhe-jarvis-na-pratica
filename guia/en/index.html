How it works

The brain thinks. The rest belongs to Jarvis.

Think of a company: the brain is the employee you hire, and Jarvis is the office, with the rules on the wall. Changing employees does not change the rules.

You ask→Jarvis adds memory and rules→the brain decides→the safety lock checks→it runs in a sandbox→answer

🧠 The brain only decides

It receives the conversation and says, “I want to check the disk” or “I want to delete the logs folder.” It cannot touch anything on the machine.

🔒 Jarvis owns the safety lock

Think of it as a gatekeeper for commands sent to the machine: read-only requests (check disk space, list folders) go straight through; any other command waits for your OK; and anything irreversible (shut down or restart the machine, delete everything, act as an administrator) is blocked even with your OK. Any file you ask it to create or edit stays inside the working folder.

📦 The sandbox

Approved commands run in an offline sandbox, restricted to the working folder (Docker is the program that creates this sandbox). If anything goes wrong, it stays inside.

📒 Memory and activity log

What you ask Jarvis to remember is stored by Jarvis, not the AI. Every command is recorded, whether approved or not.

💬 What it is like to use day to day

Through Telegram on your phone: send a message, get a reply, and when you ask it to change something, it asks “Posso rodar este comando?” (May I run this command?) and waits for your /sim (yes) or /nao (no). Or use the command screen on the machine itself (the terminal), in the same way. See Using Jarvis through Telegram.

⚠️ What this does not promise

It cannot read your email, WhatsApp, or other computer folders. In the sandbox, it can only see the working folder.

For a shop’s day-to-day work, it does not talk to your customers, read a spreadsheet on your PC (only files you put in its working folder), or access your WhatsApp.

It does not make any AI smarter: a weak brain can still give the wrong answer. What is guaranteed is protection: even if it makes a mistake, it cannot delete or restart anything without going through the safety lock. Using Jarvis through Telegram

Talk to your Jarvis on your phone

Jarvis lives on your computer or server, and you can talk to it through Telegram from anywhere. Only people on your owner list can talk to it; everyone else is ignored.

1. Create your bot

In Telegram, open @BotFather (the official bot that creates bots), send /newbot, and choose a name. It gives you a token: your bot’s key, a long code. Keep it safe and do not show it to anyone. Anyone with the token can control your bot.

2. Ask your AI to connect it

Tell your AI (the same one from “Get started”): “enable Jarvis in Practice’s Telegram mode, but do not ask me for the token in the conversation.” It creates the configuration file and tells you to run a command such as nano .env, which opens that file in the server terminal. Paste the token there (in the terminal, paste with Ctrl+Shift+V or right-click), save with Ctrl+O and Enter, exit with Ctrl+X, and the AI starts the service. It starts again automatically if the machine restarts.

Never paste the token into a conversation with the AI or any other chat.

3. Set yourself as the owner

Send /id to your bot. It replies with your Telegram number. The AI adds that number to the owner list. Until then, the bot will not reply to anyone.

4. Chat and switch AIs whenever you want

Make your request as usual. Before changing anything, the bot asks and waits for /sim (yes) or /nao (no) (2 minutes; /yes and /no also work). To see which brain is thinking, send /cerebro. To switch, send /cerebro claude, /cerebro codex, or /cerebro openrouter (the last one is pay per use). The memory and safety lock stay the same.

What was proven: with a simulated Telegram, outsiders are ignored (even when an owner is registered) and logged; the owner gets through; deleting asks for OK; /nao keeps the folder, /sim deletes it, and the brain switch remembers what was said earlier. With real Telegram, our test bot connected to the server and identified itself (record in results); the real conversation on a phone has not been tested yet.

Common questions

Before you get started

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Paste the request, read the plan the AI shows you, and say yes or no. The AI does the installation.

How much does it cost?

The project is free. With Claude Code or Codex, use the subscription you already have. With OpenRouter, you pay only for what you use, in cents.

Do I need all three brains?

No. One is enough. All three are there so you can switch whenever you want.

Does it work on my Windows PC or Mac?

We tested it on a Linux server. In step 1 of “Get started,” your AI checks your machine and tells you whether it will work.

Can the AI delete something without me seeing?

Not through this setup. The brain does not run anything: Jarvis does, after the safety lock check. Every command that does more than read waits for your OK, irreversible actions are blocked, and files it creates stay inside the working folder.

Does it work through Telegram?

Telegram mode is ready and was tested this way: 7 tests with a simulated Telegram (with an owner registered, outsiders are ignored and logged; the owner gets through; deleting asks for OK, /nao keeps the folder, /sim deletes it, and switching brains preserves memory), plus the real bot running on our server and identifying itself to Telegram. Testing a real conversation on a phone is next. The measurements for all three brains were taken through the terminal. See Using Jarvis through Telegram.

What if I already use INTELECTO?

This project does not change your INTELECTO. It only adds the three brains on top.
